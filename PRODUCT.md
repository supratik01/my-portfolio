# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three primary audiences, ranked by frequency:

1. **Recruiters / HR screeners** — 30-second scan, looking for title, tenure, and recognisable company names. Low technical depth; respond to clarity and authority.
2. **Hiring managers / tech leads** — evaluate depth, project quality, and signal-to-noise. They've seen hundreds of portfolios and are suspicious of polish that hides thin substance.
3. **Potential clients** — freelance or contract work; need confidence that Supratik can own a problem end-to-end and deliver.

All three arrive with limited time and moderate scepticism. The visit is a trust-building exercise, not a discovery session.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Supratik Das — Full Stack Developer, 7+ years, Hyderabad — deployed at bytefront.dev. The portfolio's job is to convert a visit into a message, a call, or a referral within one scroll. Success is not time-on-page; it is action taken.

## Brand Personality

Calm. Credible. Precise.

The tone is a senior engineer who doesn't need to oversell: confident without bravado, substantive without jargon-dumping. Visually quiet but never timid. The design earns trust before the words do.

## Anti-references

- Cookie-cutter developer portfolios built on Bootstrap or Webflow templates — same layout, same card grid, same "Here are my skills" section.
- Over-animated agency sites where scroll-jacking and particles overshadow the actual person.
- Neon cyberpunk dark themes (purple-on-black, glitch effects) — the Dev.to "first dark portfolio" genre.
- SaaS marketing landing pages: hero-metric stats boxes, gradient CTAs, identical icon-card grids — looks like a product, not a person.

## Design Principles

1. **Substance earns the design.** Every decorative element must serve content. If it doesn't help a visitor make a decision, remove it.
2. **Confidence through restraint.** Quiet whitespace and precise type signal seniority better than effects. Decoration is available; it is chosen sparingly.
3. **One clear ask per section.** No section competes with the next. Each fold has one dominant idea and one action.
4. **Show, don't label.** Project work is evidence. Stats are evidence. Copy that restates what the layout already communicates is dead weight.
5. **Credibility before delight.** Micro-interactions and motion are welcome only after the structure is solid. Delight is the last layer, not the first.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum; prefer AAA contrast ratios on body text.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all animations.
- No color-only communication; every state has a non-color signal.
